> **Prefill is compute-bound, so dense models are crippled at it on pop — and the rule now holds outside gemma4 (2026-08-15)**. `authoritative-20260731/README.md` established that prefill scales inversely with **active** parameters at a median **~11.3 TFLOP/s** effective (spread 10.1-14.7, i.e. ±40%), fitted entirely on gemma4 variants. Two out-of-family dense models now land in band, from opencode session observation on pop:
>
> | Model | Arch | Active | Predicted prefill | Observed |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | `qwen3.8:27b` | `qwen3_5`, dense | 27.8 B | 182-264 tok/s | consistently <300 |
> | `glimmer:coding-30b` / `muse-glimmer:30b-mlx` | `muse_glimmer`, dense | 32.3 B | 156-227 tok/s | consistently <300 |
> | MoE reference (measured, gemma4) | MoE | 4.0 B | 1,260-1,840 tok/s | 1,372-1,418 |
>
> **Why the asymmetry**: prefill runs a full forward pass per prompt token, so its cost tracks *active* params × tokens — a 27.8 B dense model does ~7× the FLOPs per token of a 4 B-active MoE. Decode is bandwidth-bound (one token at a time, cost tracks total weight bytes read), where MoE's edge is far smaller. **MTP helps decode only** — emitting more tokens per weight pass does nothing for prefill. That is exactly why `qwen3.8:27b-mlx` looks respectable on decode (~50 tok/s, 0.65× the MoEs) and terrible on prefill (~7× worse).
>
> **Operational consequence, and it is the strongest argument against dense on pop**: for long-context agentic work prefill dominates. A ~27K-token Claude-Code-scale prompt costs **~2 minutes** at 200 tok/s versus **~18 seconds** at 1,500. The decode comparison understates the gap; this is where dense actually loses. It also reframes the `codestral:22b` retirement (`INFO-1090`) — "bandwidth-bound" was the decode half of the story; the compute-bound prefill half is the larger penalty.
>
> **Status of these two rows**: predicted + session-corroborated, **not harness-measured**. The prediction is a ±40% rule of thumb and the observation is an impression from live use, not an instrumented run. `prefill-size-breakdown.py` (random per-invocation salt, 500/2k/8k ladder) is still what converts this into a matrix-grade number — see Next steps. What the two points *do* establish is that the inverse-active-params rule generalizes past the single family it was fitted on.
>
> **`qwen3.8:27b-mlx` measured (2026-08-15, TASK-1189)** — 4 runs of one config, 108/108 usable, 0 truncated, 0 empty. Agentic workload, `num_ctx=32768`, `num_predict=16384`, C=1..3, NP=3, ollama 0.32.13, `flash_attention=1`, `kv_cache_type=q8_0`.
>
> | Run | Clock | Decode C=1 (p50 `gen_tps`) | Agg C=1 | Agg C=3 | TTFT C=1 | TTFA C=1 | Resident |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | pos1 | 09:50 | **51.7** | 45.72 | 43.33 | 0.185s | 8.57s | 24 GB |
> | rep1 | 10:48 | 48.8 | 44.95 | 40.76 | 0.186s | 8.50s | 25 GB |
> | rep2 | 11:09 | 46.7 | 42.28 | 41.54 | 0.181s | 10.57s | 28 GB |
> | cold restart | 11:30 | **50.3** | 45.68 | 40.22 | 0.194s | 11.08s | 29 GB |
>
> **1. Decode degrades ~10% under sustained load and a server restart recovers it.** Three back-to-back runs of a byte-identical config fell monotonically 51.7 → 48.8 → 46.7 (−9.7% in ~80 min), steeper than the −8.3%-over-2h50m thermal drift documented in PR #58. Restarting the ollama LaunchAgent — five minutes, no cooldown period — brought it back to 50.3. **Thermal soak does not recover in five minutes; server state does.** So this is reversible server/cache state, not heat, and the useful figure is a pair: **~50-52 tok/s on a fresh server, ~47 after an hour-plus of sustained benchmarking**. Anyone quoting a single number for this tag should say which. Related open item: [[TASK-1135]] (MLX recurrent cache leak on 0.32.x) — residency also climbed 24 → 29 GB, though those samples were taken at different points within each run so the growth may be intra-run rather than across runs; not clean evidence on its own.
>
> **2. MTP is active and accepting.** At 24-29 GB resident against pop's ~570 GB/s measured streaming peak, a purely bandwidth-bound dense decode caps near 20-24 tok/s. Measured 46.7-51.7 is 2.1-2.5× that ceiling, only possible by emitting more than one token per weight pass. Same inference the `gemma4:31b-nvfp4` row made. This is what keeps a 27 B *dense* model competitive at all here.
>
> **3. MLX still does not continuous-batch, and on a dense model it is punishing.** Aggregate throughput is flat-to-declining across concurrency in every run (e.g. pos1: 45.7 → 44.8 → 43.3) while C=3 P50 TTFT reaches 39-62s and P95 hits 120s. Requests queue rather than batch — the same finding as the 2026-07-13 MoE matrix, but far worse in absolute latency because each queued request now waits behind a 27 B dense decode instead of a ~3 B-active one. **Disqualifying for concurrent serving regardless of decode rate.**
>
> **4. Dense beats the current daily driver, loses to the small MoEs.** Identical agentic-32K / C=1 / p50-`gen_tps` methodology: `nemotron3:33b` 79, `north-mini` 73, `qwen3-coder:30b` 67, **`qwen3.8:27b-mlx` ~50**, `qwen3.6:35b-mlx` 41.4. So ~1.2× the current pop daily driver but ~0.65× the fastest MoE. **INFO-1090's prefer-MoE guidance survives on decode throughput** — by a smaller margin than the codestral-era numbers implied, and against a model that is far more capable per token, but it survives.
>
> **5. Thinking dominates perceived latency.** TTFT is 0.18-0.19s but **TTFA (first *answer* token, past the thinking block) is 8.5-11.1s**. Thinking is on by default for this family. For interactive use that ~9-11s gap is the number a user actually feels.
>
> **Not answered, deliberately.** (a) **Prefill** — the harness's `server_prefill_s` implies ~1,300-1,500 tok/s, but at 177-token prompts with `repeats=3` on index-salted prompts, i.e. squarely inside the slot-cache trap documented below; the min-time sample implies 2,461 tok/s, which is the tell. **The prefill note above is independent confirmation that this harness figure is wrong**: a 27.8 B dense model cannot prefill at MoE rates, and session observation puts it under 300 tok/s — roughly 5-7× below what the confounded harness number claims. A real figure needs `prefill-size-breakdown.py` with its random salt. (b) **MLX vs GGUF** — the ABBA run was abandoned after the GGUF row proved ~3× slower in wall clock (~35 min vs ~14); the partial row was killed and discarded, writing no results dir. Do not infer the format delta from the capability-probe numbers (~68 vs ~25 tok/s), which were single short prompts, not this workload. (c) **Long-context memory** at 128K/256K — not run; the 16-of-64 KV-layer question is still open.

>
> Architecture facts, from Qwen's model card and the tag's `config.json`:
>
> - **Dense, not MoE** — 27 B params, all active every token. No `num_experts`/`experts_per_tok` in `text_config`; single FFN at `intermediate_size: 17408`. This is the first dense >12 B model to enter the candidate set since `codestral:22b` was retired for being bandwidth-bound (`INFO-1090`), so treat the decode figure as the open question, not a formality.
> - **Hybrid linear/full attention**, 64 layers, layout `16 × (3 × Gated DeltaNet→FFN → 1 × Gated Attention→FFN)`; `full_attention_interval: 4`, so only **16 of 64 layers carry a KV cache**. Gated DeltaNet: 48 V / 16 QK heads, head_dim 128. Gated Attention: 24 Q / 4 KV heads (GQA), head_dim 256, partial rotary 0.25. Hidden 5120, vocab 248,320.
> - **MTP head present** (`mtp_num_hidden_layers: 1`, `mtp_use_dedicated_embeddings: false` — shared embeddings, same pattern as `gemma4:12b-mlx`).
> - **Tag layout**: `27b-mlx` and `27b-nvfp4` share digest `5642e97495e1` (18 GB) — `-mlx` is an alias for the NVFP4 build (`W4A16_NVFP4`, group_size 16, from bf16). `27b-mxfp8` is 32 GB. The GGUF sibling `27b-q4_K_M` (`25b843619e94`) is **also 18 GB**, which makes this the cleanest same-size MLX-vs-GGUF A/B available — the format axis is confounded by size in most existing rows here.
> - **Recommended sampling params** (Qwen's, per mode): thinking — `temperature=1.0, top_p=0.95, top_k=20, min_p=0.0, presence_penalty=0.0, repetition_penalty=1.0`; instruct/non-thinking — `temperature=0.7, top_p=0.80, top_k=20, min_p=0.0, presence_penalty=1.5, repetition_penalty=1.0`. Thinking is **on by default**. Also `reasoning_effort` (`xhigh` default / `medium` / `low`) and `preserve_thinking` (on by default). Qwen notes lower reasoning effort does *not* reliably cut total multi-turn agentic time despite faster per-turn responses. Beyond 262K, YaRN `factor: 4.0` — vLLM/SGLang only, not exposed by Ollama.
> - **~~The `-mlx` tag is probably text-only~~ — TESTED 2026-08-15, vision works on both tags.** See the capability-probe paragraph above. The prediction was wrong; ollama#16700's "MLX runner is currently text only" no longer holds on 0.32.13.
> - **The two tags are NOT the equal-size control this note originally claimed.** `ollama list` reports 17 GB for both, but they differ in parameter count (MLX 27.8 B counted inline vs GGUF 27.3 B + a separate 460.73M `clip` projector) and, more importantly, in **residency: MLX 24-29 GB vs GGUF 18 GB** at the same 32K context. Still the tightest MLX/GGUF pair available here, but treat any future comparison as format-plus-quantization-plus-residency, not format alone.
> - **Inherited caveats**: the engine rule in the Recommendation matrix below applies to this tag on arrival — MLX path silently drops `format` JSON schemas (`INFO-1127`, root-caused to `x/mlxrunner/client.go` never forwarding `format`; constrained sampling is simply unimplemented for the MLX engine), plus whitespace corruption in tool-call content (`BUG-1067`). It is not exempt because it is a new family. `tools` *is* honored on the MLX engine; only `format` is dropped. The 18 GB q4_K_M sibling is the row to prefer for code-writing, schema-constrained output, and anything involving images, until the MLX path is re-cleared.
>
> **Small-MoE agentic matrix added (2026-07-13)**: four sub-35B models (`north-mini-code-1.0`, `nemotron3:33b`, `qwen3-coder:30b`, `hermes3:8b`) benchmarked against the `qwen3.6:35b-mlx` daily driver under one env (agentic workload, `num_ctx=32768`, `C=1..3`, NP=3) — see `report-2026-07-13-moe-matrix/`. **All four are faster than the baseline** (1.6-1.9× single-stream decode, 1.9-2.4× aggregate at C=3). Two takeaways: (1) **MLX does not continuous-batch** — the MLX models (`north-mini`, `qwen3.6`) show aggregate ≈ single-stream, while the GGUF/llama.cpp models scale with concurrency (`hermes3` +40%, `qwen3-coder` +33%); (2) the `qwen3.6:35b-mlx` **decode figure differs by workload** — 41 tok/s under this agentic-32K run vs the ~88-111 editpred-ladder number above. This is not a thinking artifact (decode t/s is content-independent) and not a 32K penalty (`north-mini`, same backend/ctx/active-params, hit 73). Both numbers are kept, workload-labelled; the canonical daily-driver decode rate warrants a follow-up. `laguna-xs-2.1` was in scope but dropped — upstream-acknowledged macOS/Metal empty-output bug (revisit after the fix).
